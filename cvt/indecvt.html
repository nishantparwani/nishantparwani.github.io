🔥 PRIORITY 1

Fourier Transform

You said you don't know this — read every line below. These appear on every single paper.

— MUST MEMORISE Fourier Transform — Complete Formula Sheet
DEFINITION (Full FT): $$F(\lambda) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} f(x)\, e^{i\lambda x}\, dx$$ INVERSE: $$f(x) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} F(\lambda)\, e^{-i\lambda x}\, d\lambda$$
Transform Definition Inverse
Fourier Cosine \(F_c\) \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty f(x)\cos(\lambda x)\,dx\) \(\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\infty F_c(\lambda)\cos(\lambda x)\,d\lambda\)
Fourier Sine \(F_s\) \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty f(x)\sin(\lambda x)\,dx\) \(\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\infty F_s(\lambda)\sin(\lambda x)\,d\lambda\)
KEY TRICK: After computing \(F(\lambda)\), apply the inverse FT at a convenient point \(x = x_0\) where \(f(x_0)\) is known — this lets you deduce the value of famous integrals like \(\int_0^\infty \frac{\sin x}{x}dx\).
WHEN to use Cosine vs Sine transform: Use \(F_c\) for even functions / when the integral has \(\cos\). Use \(F_s\) for odd functions / when the integral has \(\sin\).
FT-1 PYQ EVERY PAPER Find Fourier transform of \(f(x) = k\) for \(|x| \le a\), and \(0\) for \(|x| > a\). Hence deduce \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{2}\). [6m]
⚡ EVERY SINGLE PAPER. Two steps: compute FT, then invert at x = 0.
PATTERN: Rectangular pulse → \(\sin(\lambda a)/\lambda\) type result. Invert at a point to deduce an integral.
Step 1 — Compute FT: $$F(\lambda) = \int_{-a}^{a} k\, e^{i\lambda x}\,dx = k\left[\frac{e^{i\lambda x}}{i\lambda}\right]_{-a}^{a} = \frac{k}{i\lambda}\left(e^{i\lambda a} - e^{-i\lambda a}\right)$$
Use \(e^{i\theta} - e^{-i\theta} = 2i\sin\theta\): $$\boxed{F(\lambda) = \frac{2k\sin(\lambda a)}{\lambda}}$$ At \(\lambda = 0\): \(F(0) = 2ka\) (by L'Hôpital or direct).
Step 2 — Deduce the integral: Apply inverse FT at \(x = 0\) (a point of continuity where \(f(0) = k\)): $$f(0) = k = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} F(\lambda)\,e^{0}\,d\lambda = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{2k\sin(\lambda a)}{\lambda}\,d\lambda$$
Since \(\frac{\sin(\lambda a)}{\lambda}\) is even: $$k = \frac{k}{\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\sin(\lambda a)}{\lambda}\,d\lambda = \frac{2k}{\pi}\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin(\lambda a)}{\lambda}\,d\lambda$$ $$\Rightarrow \int_0^\infty\frac{\sin(\lambda a)}{\lambda}\,d\lambda = \frac{\pi}{2}$$
Substitute \(\lambda a = x\) (let \(x = \lambda a\), \(dx = a\,d\lambda\)): $$\int_0^\infty\frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{2}$$
$$\boxed{F(\lambda) = \frac{2k\sin(\lambda a)}{\lambda}}, \qquad \int_0^\infty\frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{2}$$
POINT TO NOTE: The substitution at the end is just renaming \(\lambda a \to x\). The key step is inverting at \(x = 0\) — at a point of continuity, the inverse formula gives exactly \(f(0)\). Don't invert at a jump discontinuity.
FT-2 PYQ 2022-23 Re-exam Find Fourier sine transform of \(f(x) = e^{-x}\), \(x \ge 0\). Deduce \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty\frac{x\sin mx}{1+x^2}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{2}e^{-m}\). [8m]
⚡ IBP twice trick. Deduction by inversion. One of the most common 8-mark FT questions.
PATTERN (IBP twice trick): For integrals of \(e^{-ax}\sin(\lambda x)\) or \(e^{-ax}\cos(\lambda x)\), integrate by parts twice. After two IBPs you get the same integral on the RHS — solve algebraically: \(I(1+\lambda^2) = \lambda\).
Compute \(F_s(\lambda)\): $$F_s(\lambda) = \int_0^\infty e^{-x}\sin(\lambda x)\,dx$$
IBP: let \(u = \sin(\lambda x),\; dv = e^{-x}dx\): $$= \left[-e^{-x}\sin(\lambda x)\right]_0^\infty + \lambda\int_0^\infty e^{-x}\cos(\lambda x)\,dx = 0 + \lambda\int_0^\infty e^{-x}\cos(\lambda x)\,dx$$
IBP again on the new integral: let \(u = \cos(\lambda x),\; dv = e^{-x}dx\): $$= \lambda\left(\left[-e^{-x}\cos(\lambda x)\right]_0^\infty - \lambda\int_0^\infty e^{-x}\sin(\lambda x)\,dx\right) = \lambda\left(1 - \lambda F_s(\lambda)\right)$$
Solve: \(F_s(\lambda) = \lambda - \lambda^2 F_s(\lambda) \Rightarrow F_s(\lambda)(1+\lambda^2) = \lambda\): $$\boxed{F_s(\lambda) = \frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda^2}}$$
Deduce the integral: Inverse sine transform: $$e^{-x} = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\infty F_s(\lambda)\sin(\lambda x)\,d\lambda = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\infty\frac{\lambda\sin(\lambda x)}{1+\lambda^2}\,d\lambda$$
Rename \(\lambda \to x\) and \(x \to m\): $$e^{-m} = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\infty\frac{x\sin(mx)}{1+x^2}\,dx$$
$$\boxed{F_s(\lambda) = \frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda^2}, \qquad \int_0^\infty\frac{x\sin mx}{1+x^2}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{2}e^{-m}}$$
POINT TO NOTE: The "rename variables" step at the end is not doing any new calculation — you just rename \(\lambda \to x\) and \(x \to m\) in the inversion formula. The inversion formula holds because \(f(x) = e^{-x}\) is continuous and integrable on \([0,\infty)\).
FT-3 PYQ / Tutorial Find Fourier cosine transform of \(f(x) = e^{-ax}\), \(a > 0\). Deduce \(\displaystyle\int_0^\infty\frac{\cos\lambda x}{a^2+\lambda^2}\,d\lambda = \frac{\pi}{2a}e^{-ax}\). [6m]
⚡ Identical IBP trick as FT-2 but with cosine. Companion question — appears alongside or as alternative.
PATTERN: Same IBP twice technique. \(F_c = a/(a^2+\lambda^2)\).
Compute \(F_c(\lambda)\): $$F_c(\lambda) = \int_0^\infty e^{-ax}\cos(\lambda x)\,dx$$ IBP twice (same as FT-2): $$F_c(\lambda) = \frac{a}{a^2 + \lambda^2}$$
Inversion: $$e^{-ax} = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\infty F_c(\lambda)\cos(\lambda x)\,d\lambda = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\infty\frac{a\cos(\lambda x)}{a^2+\lambda^2}\,d\lambda$$ Divide both sides by \(\frac{2a}{\pi}\): $$\int_0^\infty\frac{\cos\lambda x}{a^2+\lambda^2}\,d\lambda = \frac{\pi}{2a}e^{-ax}$$
$$F_c(\lambda) = \frac{a}{a^2+\lambda^2}, \qquad \int_0^\infty\frac{\cos\lambda x}{a^2+\lambda^2}\,d\lambda = \frac{\pi}{2a}e^{-ax}$$
POINT TO NOTE: This result (\(\int_0^\infty \frac{\cos\lambda x}{a^2+\lambda^2}d\lambda\)) appears as a sub-result in Parseval's theorem problems too — memorise it.
FT-4 PYQ 2022-23, 2023-24 Find Fourier cosine transform of \(f(x) = x\) for \(0 < x \le 1\),\; \(2-x\) for \(1 < x \le 2\),\; \(0\) for \(x > 2\). [6m]
⚡ Triangle function — appears in pairs of papers. Break into two IBP integrals, combine.
PATTERN (piecewise function): Split the FT integral at the breakpoints. Do IBP on each piece, then combine carefully.
$$F_c(\lambda) = \int_0^1 x\cos(\lambda x)\,dx + \int_1^2(2-x)\cos(\lambda x)\,dx$$
First integral (IBP: \(u = x,\; dv = \cos\lambda x\,dx\)): $$I_1 = \left[\frac{x\sin\lambda x}{\lambda}\right]_0^1 - \frac{1}{\lambda}\int_0^1\sin\lambda x\,dx = \frac{\sin\lambda}{\lambda} + \frac{1}{\lambda}\left[\frac{\cos\lambda x}{\lambda}\right]_0^1 = \frac{\sin\lambda}{\lambda} + \frac{\cos\lambda - 1}{\lambda^2}$$
Second integral (IBP: \(u = 2-x,\; dv = \cos\lambda x\,dx\)): $$I_2 = \left[\frac{(2-x)\sin\lambda x}{\lambda}\right]_1^2 + \frac{1}{\lambda}\int_1^2\sin\lambda x\,dx = -\frac{\sin\lambda}{\lambda} + \frac{1}{\lambda}\left[-\frac{\cos\lambda x}{\lambda}\right]_1^2$$ $$= -\frac{\sin\lambda}{\lambda} + \frac{\cos\lambda - \cos 2\lambda}{\lambda^2}$$
Add \(I_1 + I_2\): $$F_c(\lambda) = \frac{2\cos\lambda - 1 - \cos 2\lambda}{\lambda^2}$$ Use \(\cos 2\lambda = 2\cos^2\lambda - 1\), so \(2\cos\lambda - 1 - \cos 2\lambda = 2\cos\lambda - 1 - (2\cos^2\lambda - 1) = 2\cos\lambda - 2\cos^2\lambda = 2\cos\lambda(1-\cos\lambda)\). Also: \(1 - \cos\lambda = 2\sin^2(\lambda/2)\), \(\cos\lambda = 1 - 2\sin^2(\lambda/2)\), so numerator \(= 2(1-\cos\lambda) = 4\sin^2(\lambda/2)\).
$$F_c(\lambda) = \frac{2(1-\cos\lambda)}{\lambda^2}$$
POINT TO NOTE: Triangle function always gives a \(\frac{1-\cos\lambda}{\lambda^2}\) type result. Recognise the pattern — if you see a triangular piecewise function, expect this answer.
🔥 PRIORITY 2

Convolution Theorem (Laplace)

Convolution theorem links product of transforms to a convolution integral. Read the formula, then the example.

— MUST MEMORISE Convolution Theorem — Formula
CONVOLUTION DEFINITION: $$(f * g)(t) = \int_0^t f(\tau)\,g(t-\tau)\,d\tau$$ CONVOLUTION THEOREM: $$\mathcal{L}\{f * g\} = F(s)\cdot G(s)$$ $$\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{F(s)\cdot G(s)\} = \int_0^t f(\tau)\,g(t-\tau)\,d\tau$$
HOW TO USE: When you need ILT of a product \(F(s) \cdot G(s)\), split it as \(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{F\}\) and \(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{G\}\), then compute the convolution integral.
STANDARD ILT PAIRS (needed inside convolution):
\(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{1/s^n\} = t^{n-1}/(n-1)!\)  ·  \(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{1/(s+a)^n\} = t^{n-1}e^{-at}/(n-1)!\)
\(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{1/s^2\} = t\)  ·  \(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{1/(s+1)^2\} = te^{-t}\)
CV-1 Tutorial T11 Find \(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\!\left\{\dfrac{1}{s^2(s+1)^2}\right\}\) using convolution theorem. [6m]
⚡ The standard convolution ILT question. Know how to set up and evaluate the integral step by step.
PATTERN: Write as product \(\frac{1}{s^2} \cdot \frac{1}{(s+1)^2}\). ILT of each factor: \(f(\tau) = \tau\) and \(g(\tau) = \tau e^{-\tau}\). Then compute \(\int_0^t f(\tau)g(t-\tau)\,d\tau\).
Identify: \(F(s) = 1/s^2 \Rightarrow f(\tau) = \tau\); \quad G(s) = 1/(s+1)^2 \Rightarrow g(\tau) = \tau e^{-\tau}\).
By convolution theorem: $$h(t) = \int_0^t \tau \cdot (t-\tau)e^{-(t-\tau)}\,d\tau = e^{-t}\int_0^t \tau(t-\tau)e^{\tau}\,d\tau$$
Expand \(\tau(t-\tau) = t\tau - \tau^2\): $$= e^{-t}\left[t\int_0^t \tau e^{\tau}\,d\tau - \int_0^t \tau^2 e^\tau\,d\tau\right]$$
Use IBP: \(\int \tau e^\tau d\tau = (\tau-1)e^\tau + C\) and \(\int \tau^2 e^\tau d\tau = (\tau^2-2\tau+2)e^\tau + C\). $$= e^{-t}\left[t\left[(t-1)e^t + 1\right] - \left[(t^2-2t+2)e^t - 2\right]\right]$$ $$= e^{-t}\left[t(t-1)e^t + t - (t^2-2t+2)e^t + 2\right]$$
Collect \(e^t\) terms: \([t(t-1) - (t^2-2t+2)]e^t = [t^2-t-t^2+2t-2]e^t = (t-2)e^t\) $$= e^{-t}\left[(t-2)e^t + t + 2\right] = (t-2) + (t+2)e^{-t}$$
$$\mathcal{L}^{-1}\!\left\{\frac{1}{s^2(s+1)^2}\right\} = t - 2 + (t+2)e^{-t}$$
POINT TO NOTE: Always factor out \(e^{-t}\) from the integral before computing IBP — it simplifies the algebra greatly. The final answer should have no remaining integral sign.
⚠ Do NOT just write the answer as \(te^{-t} + 2e^{-t} + t - 2\) without showing the convolution integral setup — full marks require the integral.
FT-5 PYQ 2024-25 Using Parseval's theorem for Fourier transforms, show that \(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{(a^2+x^2)^2} = \frac{\pi}{2a^3}\) (\(a > 0\)). [6m]
⚡ Parseval's theorem — a cousin of convolution. Appears as a hard question; read the method once.
PARSEVAL'S THEOREM (FT version): $$\int_{-\infty}^\infty |f(x)|^2\,dx = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty |F(\lambda)|^2\,d\lambda$$ STRATEGY: Pick a function whose FT is known and whose square gives the desired integrand.
Choose \(f(x) = e^{-a|x|}\). Then (standard result from FT-3 extended to full line): $$F(\lambda) = \int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-a|x|}e^{i\lambda x}\,dx = \frac{2a}{a^2+\lambda^2}$$
Apply Parseval's: $$\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-2a|x|}\,dx = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^\infty \frac{4a^2}{(a^2+\lambda^2)^2}\,d\lambda$$
LHS: \(\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^\infty e^{-2a|x|}\,dx = 2\int_0^\infty e^{-2ax}\,dx = 2\cdot\frac{1}{2a} = \frac{1}{a}\)
RHS: \(\dfrac{4a^2}{2\pi}\cdot 2\displaystyle\int_0^\infty\frac{d\lambda}{(a^2+\lambda^2)^2} = \frac{4a^2}{\pi}\cdot I\)
So: \(\dfrac{1}{a} = \dfrac{4a^2}{\pi}\cdot I \Rightarrow I = \dfrac{\pi}{4a^3}\). But the question asks for \(\int_{-\infty}^\infty\), which is \(2I = \dfrac{\pi}{2a^3}\). ✓
$$\int_{-\infty}^\infty \frac{dx}{(a^2+x^2)^2} = \frac{\pi}{2a^3}$$
POINT TO NOTE: Parseval's = "energy in time domain equals energy in frequency domain (scaled)". The key is choosing the right \(f(x)\) — pick one whose FT you already know.

Inverse Laplace Transform

Three question types. Every ILT exam question is one of these three patterns.

— KEY PAIRS ILT Standard Pairs to Remember
\(F(s)\)\(f(t) = \mathcal{L}^{-1}\{F\}\)
\(1/s\)\(1\)
\(1/s^2\)\(t\)
\(1/s^n\)\(t^{n-1}/(n-1)!\)
\(1/(s+a)\)\(e^{-at}\)
\(1/(s+a)^2\)\(te^{-at}\)
\(s/(s^2+\omega^2)\)\(\cos\omega t\)
\(\omega/(s^2+\omega^2)\)\(\sin\omega t\)
\((s-a)/[(s-a)^2+b^2]\)\(e^{at}\cos bt\)
\(b/[(s-a)^2+b^2]\)\(e^{at}\sin bt\)
ILT-1 PYQ 2022-23 & 2024-25 Find \(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\!\left\{\dfrac{s+2}{s^2(s+3)}\right\}\) [6m]
⚡ TYPE: Partial fractions with repeated + simple poles. Appears every year.
PATTERN: \(\dfrac{f(s)}{s^2(s+b)}\) → split into \(\dfrac{A}{s} + \dfrac{B}{s^2} + \dfrac{C}{s+b}\). Find A, B, C by substituting convenient \(s\) values.
Write: \(\dfrac{s+2}{s^2(s+3)} = \dfrac{A}{s}+\dfrac{B}{s^2}+\dfrac{C}{s+3}\)
Multiply both sides by \(s^2(s+3)\): \(s+2 = As(s+3)+B(s+3)+Cs^2\)
Put \(s=0\): \(2 = 3B \Rightarrow B = \dfrac{2}{3}\)
Put \(s=-3\): \(-1 = 9C \Rightarrow C = -\dfrac{1}{9}\)
Compare \(s^2\) coefficients: \(0 = A+C \Rightarrow A = \dfrac{1}{9}\)
Invert: \(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{A/s\} = A,\quad \mathcal{L}^{-1}\{B/s^2\} = Bt,\quad \mathcal{L}^{-1}\{C/(s+3)\} = Ce^{-3t}\)
$$f(t)=\frac{1}{9}+\frac{2t}{3}-\frac{e^{-3t}}{9}$$
POINT TO NOTE: For \(B/s^2\): ILT is \(Bt\) (not just \(B\)). For \(A/s\): ILT is \(A \cdot 1\). Very common mistake to confuse these.
ILT-2 PYQ 2022-23 & 2024-25 Find \(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\!\left\{\dfrac{5s^2+8s-1}{(s+3)(s^2+1)}\right\}\) [6m]
⚡ TYPE: Quadratic irreducible factor → gives sin and cos in answer.
PATTERN: Quadratic factor \((s^2+\omega^2)\): use \(\dfrac{Bs+C}{s^2+\omega^2}\) in PF (not two separate terms). This inverts to \(B\cos\omega t + \dfrac{C}{\omega}\sin\omega t\).
Write: \(\dfrac{5s^2+8s-1}{(s+3)(s^2+1)} = \dfrac{A}{s+3}+\dfrac{Bs+C}{s^2+1}\)
Put \(s=-3\): \(A(9+1) = 45-24-1 = 20 \Rightarrow A = 2\)
Compare \(s^2\): \(A+B = 5 \Rightarrow B = 3\)
Compare constants: \(A+3C = -1 \Rightarrow 2+3C=-1 \Rightarrow C = -1\)
Invert: \(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\!\left\{\dfrac{2}{s+3}\right\} = 2e^{-3t}\), \quad \mathcal{L}^{-1}\!\left\{\dfrac{3s-1}{s^2+1}\right\} = 3\cos t - \sin t\)
$$f(t) = 2e^{-3t}+3\cos t-\sin t$$
POINT TO NOTE: \(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{(Bs+C)/(s^2+1)\} = B\cos t + C\sin t\) (since \(\omega = 1\), the \(C/\omega = C\) here). If \(\omega \ne 1\), write \(\dfrac{C}{\omega}\cdot\dfrac{\omega}{s^2+\omega^2}\) and ILT = \(\dfrac{C}{\omega}\sin\omega t\).
ILT-3 Tutorial T10 Find \(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\!\left\{\dfrac{6s-4}{s^2-4s+20}\right\}\) [4m]
⚡ TYPE: Irreducible quadratic with no factoring → complete the square → \(e^{at}\cos bt\) or \(e^{at}\sin bt\).
PATTERN: Complete the square: \(s^2 + ps + q = (s+p/2)^2 + (q - p^2/4)\). Split numerator to match \((s-\alpha)\) and a constant separately. Use: \(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\!\left\{\dfrac{s-a}{(s-a)^2+b^2}\right\} = e^{at}\cos bt\) and \(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\!\left\{\dfrac{b}{(s-a)^2+b^2}\right\} = e^{at}\sin bt\).
Complete square: \(s^2-4s+20 = (s-2)^2+16\)
Split numerator: \(6s-4 = 6(s-2) + 12 - 4 = 6(s-2) + 8\)
\(\dfrac{6(s-2)}{(s-2)^2+16} \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}^{-1}} 6e^{2t}\cos 4t\)
\(\dfrac{8}{(s-2)^2+16} = \dfrac{8}{4}\cdot\dfrac{4}{(s-2)^2+16} \xrightarrow{\mathcal{L}^{-1}} 2e^{2t}\sin 4t\)
$$f(t) = e^{2t}(6\cos 4t + 2\sin 4t)$$
POINT TO NOTE: When splitting numerator for \(\sin\) term: \(\dfrac{8}{(s-2)^2+16}\) — you must force it to be \(\dfrac{2 \cdot 4}{(s-2)^2+4^2}\), because the ILT formula requires \(\dfrac{b}{(s-a)^2+b^2}\) where \(b=4\). So multiply and divide by 4, pulling factor of 2 outside.
ILT-4 PYQ 2023-24, Tutorial T11 Evaluate \(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\!\left\{\dfrac{8e^{-3s}}{s^2+4}\right\}\). [4m]
⚡ 2nd shift theorem — one line question if you know the formula.
2ND SHIFT THEOREM: \(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{e^{-as}F(s)\} = f(t-a)\cdot H(t-a)\) where \(f = \mathcal{L}^{-1}\{F\}\).
Ignore \(e^{-3s}\) for now: \(\mathcal{L}^{-1}\!\left\{\dfrac{8}{s^2+4}\right\} = 8\cdot\dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{2}{s^2+4} \xrightarrow{} 4\sin 2t\)
Apply 2nd shift (replace \(t \to t-3\), multiply by \(H(t-3)\)):
$$f(t) = 4\sin 2(t-3)\cdot H(t-3)$$
POINT TO NOTE: \(H(t-3)\) means the answer is 0 for \(t < 3\) and \(4\sin 2(t-3)\) for \(t \ge 3\). You must include the Heaviside function in your final answer.
Unit 4

Full Range Fourier Series

Know the even/odd shortcut — it halves your work on every paper.

— FORMULA Fourier Series Formulae at a Glance
General period \(2\pi\): $$f(x) = \frac{a_0}{2} + \sum_{n=1}^\infty (a_n\cos nx + b_n\sin nx)$$ $$a_0 = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^\pi f\,dx,\quad a_n = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^\pi f\cos nx\,dx,\quad b_n = \frac{1}{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^\pi f\sin nx\,dx$$
TypeShortcut
EVEN function \(f(-x)=f(x)\)\(b_n = 0\). Only compute \(a_0, a_n\) (double the integral from 0 to \(\pi\))
ODD function \(f(-x)=-f(x)\)\(a_0 = a_n = 0\). Only compute \(b_n\) (double the integral from 0 to \(\pi\))
FS-1 PYQ EVERY PAPER Obtain Fourier series of \(f(x) = x^2\) in \((-\pi,\pi)\). Deduce \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}\). [8m]
⚡ Appears literally every paper. Fastest 8 marks if you know it.
Even function: \(b_n = 0\).
\(a_0 = \dfrac{2}{\pi}\displaystyle\int_0^\pi x^2\,dx = \dfrac{2}{\pi}\cdot\dfrac{\pi^3}{3} = \dfrac{2\pi^2}{3}\)
\(a_n = \dfrac{2}{\pi}\displaystyle\int_0^\pi x^2\cos nx\,dx\). IBP twice: \(= \dfrac{4(-1)^n}{n^2}\)
$$f(x) = \frac{\pi^2}{3} + \sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{4(-1)^n}{n^2}\cos nx$$
Put \(x = \pi\) (point of continuity): \(\pi^2 = \dfrac{\pi^2}{3} + \displaystyle\sum\dfrac{4(-1)^n}{n^2}\cdot(-1)^n = \dfrac{\pi^2}{3} + 4\displaystyle\sum\dfrac{1}{n^2}\)
$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}$$
POINT TO NOTE (IBP formula for \(a_n\)): \(\displaystyle\int_0^\pi x^2\cos nx\,dx = \left[\dfrac{x^2\sin nx}{n}\right]_0^\pi - \dfrac{2}{n}\int_0^\pi x\sin nx\,dx\). First term is 0. Second IBP on \(\int x\sin nx\): \(\left[-\dfrac{x\cos nx}{n}+\dfrac{\sin nx}{n^2}\right]_0^\pi = -\dfrac{\pi(-1)^n}{n}\). So \(a_n = \dfrac{2}{\pi}\cdot\dfrac{2(-1)^n}{n}\cdot\dfrac{\pi}{n} = \dfrac{4(-1)^n}{n^2}\).
FS-2 PYQ 2024-25 Construct FS of \(f(x) = |x|+1\) for \(-\pi < x < \pi\). Deduce \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{1}{(2n-1)^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{8}\). [8m]
⚡ Even function trick. Deduction by putting x = 0. Know which \(n\) terms survive.
Even: \(b_n = 0\). \(a_0 = \dfrac{2}{\pi}\displaystyle\int_0^\pi(x+1)\,dx = \dfrac{2}{\pi}\left[\dfrac{\pi^2}{2}+\pi\right] = \pi+2\)
\(a_n = \dfrac{2}{\pi}\displaystyle\int_0^\pi(x+1)\cos nx\,dx\). IBP on \(\int x\cos nx\,dx = \dfrac{x\sin nx}{n}+\dfrac{\cos nx}{n^2}\): $$a_n = \dfrac{2}{\pi}\left[\dfrac{(-1)^n-1}{n^2} + 0\right] = \dfrac{2((-1)^n-1)}{\pi n^2}$$
For even \(n\): \((-1)^n-1 = 0\), so \(a_n = 0\). For odd \(n\): \((-1)^n-1 = -2\), so \(a_n = -\dfrac{4}{\pi n^2}\).
$$f(x) = \frac{\pi+2}{2} - \frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{n\text{ odd}}\frac{\cos nx}{n^2}$$
Put \(x=0\): \(f(0) = 1\), \(\cos(n\cdot 0) = 1\): $$1 = \frac{\pi+2}{2} - \frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{k=1}^\infty\frac{1}{(2k-1)^2}$$ $$\frac{4}{\pi}\sum\frac{1}{(2k-1)^2} = \frac{\pi+2}{2}-1 = \frac{\pi}{2}$$
$$\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{1}{(2n-1)^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{8}$$
POINT TO NOTE: For deduction questions — put in a specific value of \(x\) where: (i) you know \(f(x)\) and (ii) all the \(\cos nx\) or \(\sin nx\) simplify nicely. \(x=0\) (all cosines = 1), \(x = \pi\) (all cosines = \((-1)^n\)), \(x = \pi/2\) (cosines alternately 0, ±1) are the most used.
FS-3 PYQ Q1 Every Paper Find Fourier series of \(f(x) = x\) in \((-\pi,\pi)\). [4m]
Odd: \(a_0 = a_n = 0\). Only \(b_n\): $$b_n = \frac{2}{\pi}\int_0^\pi x\sin nx\,dx = \frac{2}{\pi}\left[-\frac{x\cos nx}{n}+\frac{\sin nx}{n^2}\right]_0^\pi = \frac{2}{\pi}\cdot\frac{-\pi(-1)^n}{n} = \frac{2(-1)^{n+1}}{n}$$
$$x = 2\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}\sin nx = 2\left(\sin x - \frac{\sin 2x}{2}+\frac{\sin 3x}{3}-\cdots\right)$$
POINT TO NOTE: \((-1)^{n+1}\) means: \(+\) for \(n=1\), \(-\) for \(n=2\), \(+\) for \(n=3\), etc. At \(x = \pm\pi\) this series converges to 0 (average of left and right limits at the jump).
FS-4 PYQ 2022-23, 2023-24 Find Fourier series of \(f(x) = 2\pi - x\) in \((0,2\pi)\). [6m]
⚡ Non-symmetric interval version. Formulas change slightly: \(1/\pi \int_0^{2\pi}\).
FORMULA for \([0,2\pi]\): \(a_0 = \dfrac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f\,dx\), \(a_n = \dfrac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f\cos nx\,dx\), \(b_n = \dfrac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f\sin nx\,dx\).
\(a_0 = \dfrac{1}{\pi}\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}(2\pi-x)\,dx = \dfrac{1}{\pi}\left[2\pi x - \dfrac{x^2}{2}\right]_0^{2\pi} = \dfrac{1}{\pi}(4\pi^2 - 2\pi^2) = 2\pi\)
\(a_n = \dfrac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}(2\pi-x)\cos nx\,dx\). IBP, boundary terms vanish: \(a_n = 0\)
\(b_n = \dfrac{1}{\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}(2\pi-x)\sin nx\,dx = \dfrac{2}{n}\)
$$f(x) = \pi + \sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{2}{n}\sin nx$$
POINT TO NOTE: In \([0,2\pi]\), there's no even/odd shortcut — just compute all three coefficients. \(a_n = 0\) here because the integral of \((2\pi-x)\cos nx\) over a full period cancels.
Unit 4

Half-Range Fourier Series

Half-range = choose to extend as even (cosine series) or odd (sine series). Appears every paper.

— FORMULA Half-Range Formulae on \((0,L)\)
TypeFormulaSeries
Cosine (even extension)\(a_0=\frac{2}{L}\int_0^L f\,dx\), \(a_n=\frac{2}{L}\int_0^L f\cos\frac{n\pi x}{L}dx\)\(\frac{a_0}{2}+\sum a_n\cos\frac{n\pi x}{L}\)
Sine (odd extension)\(b_n=\frac{2}{L}\int_0^L f\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}dx\)\(\sum b_n\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}\)
HFS-1 PYQ 2024-25 & 2022-23 Obtain half-range cosine series for \(f(x) = (x-1)^2\) in \(0 < x < 1\). [6m]
⚡ Half-range cosine appears every paper. Substitution \(t = x-1\) simplifies the integral.
\(L = 1\). \(a_0 = 2\displaystyle\int_0^1(x-1)^2\,dx = 2\left[\dfrac{(x-1)^3}{3}\right]_0^1 = 2\cdot\dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{2}{3}\)
\(a_n = 2\displaystyle\int_0^1(x-1)^2\cos(n\pi x)\,dx\). Substitute \(t = x-1\) (limits \(-1 \to 0\)): $$= 2\int_{-1}^0 t^2\cos n\pi(t+1)\,dt = 2(-1)^n\int_{-1}^0 t^2\cos(n\pi t)\,dt$$ Since \(t^2\cos(n\pi t)\) is even in \(t\): \(= 4(-1)^n\int_0^1 t^2\cos(n\pi t)\,dt\)
IBP twice: \(\displaystyle\int_0^1 t^2\cos(n\pi t)\,dt = \dfrac{2(-1)^n}{n^2\pi^2}\)
\(a_n = 4(-1)^n \cdot \dfrac{2(-1)^n}{n^2\pi^2} = \dfrac{8(-1)^{2n}}{n^2\pi^2} = \dfrac{4}{n^2\pi^2}\)
$$f(x) = \frac{1}{3} + \sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{4}{n^2\pi^2}\cos(n\pi x)$$
POINT TO NOTE: After substitution \(t = x-1\), \(\cos n\pi(t+1) = \cos(n\pi t + n\pi) = \cos(n\pi t)\cos(n\pi) = (-1)^n\cos(n\pi t)\). This is the key step that removes the shift. The \((-1)^n\) cancels when squared.
HFS-2 PYQ 2022-23 Final Find half-range cosine series for \(f(x) = \sin x\) in \(0 < x < \pi\). [6m]
\(L = \pi\). \(a_0 = \dfrac{2}{\pi}\displaystyle\int_0^\pi\sin x\,dx = \dfrac{2}{\pi}[-\cos x]_0^\pi = \dfrac{2}{\pi}(1+1) = \dfrac{4}{\pi}\)
\(a_1 = \dfrac{2}{\pi}\displaystyle\int_0^\pi\sin x\cos x\,dx = \dfrac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi\sin 2x\,dx = 0\)
For \(n \ge 2\): use product formula \(\sin x\cos nx = \dfrac{1}{2}[\sin(1+n)x + \sin(1-n)x]\): $$a_n = \dfrac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\pi[\sin(n+1)x + \sin(1-n)x]\,dx = \dfrac{-1+(-1)^n}{\pi}\cdot\dfrac{2}{n^2-1} = \dfrac{-2(1+(-1)^n)}{\pi(n^2-1)}$$ Zero for odd \(n \ge 3\); for even \(n\): \(a_n = -\dfrac{4}{\pi(n^2-1)}\).
$$\sin x = \frac{2}{\pi} - \frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{\cos 2nx}{4n^2-1}$$
POINT TO NOTE: The \(n=1\) case must be handled separately because the product formula gives \(\sin(0) = 0\) in the denominator otherwise. Never forget to check \(n=1\) separately for any FS question involving products of trig functions.
Unit 4

Complex Fourier Series

— FORMULA Complex FS Formula
$$c_n = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^\pi f(x)e^{-inx}\,dx \qquad f(x) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^\infty c_n e^{inx}$$ Key result: \(\sinh[(a+in)\pi] = (-1)^n\sinh(a\pi)\) because \(\sin(n\pi) = 0\) for integer \(n\).
CFS-1 PYQ 2024-25 & 2022-23 Find complex Fourier series of \(f(x) = e^{-ax}\) in \((-\pi,\pi)\), \(a \ne 0\) real. [6m]
⚡ Appears every other paper. One formula, one integration, one simplification using \(\sinh\).
\(c_n = \dfrac{1}{2\pi}\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^\pi e^{-ax}e^{-inx}\,dx = \dfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^\pi e^{-(a+in)x}\,dx\)
\(= \dfrac{1}{2\pi}\cdot\dfrac{e^{-(a+in)x}}{-(a+in)}\Bigg|_{-\pi}^{\pi} = \dfrac{1}{2\pi(a+in)}\left[e^{(a+in)\pi} - e^{-(a+in)\pi}\right]\)
\(= \dfrac{\sinh[(a+in)\pi]}{\pi(a+in)}\)
Expand: \(\sinh[(a+in)\pi] = \sinh(a\pi)\cos(n\pi) + i\cosh(a\pi)\sin(n\pi)\). Since \(\sin(n\pi) = 0\) for integer \(n\): \(= (-1)^n\sinh(a\pi)\)
$$c_n = \frac{(-1)^n\sinh(a\pi)}{\pi(a+in)}, \qquad f(x) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^\infty\frac{(-1)^n\sinh(a\pi)}{\pi(a+in)}e^{inx}$$
POINT TO NOTE: The key simplification is that \(\sin(n\pi) = 0\) for all integers, killing the imaginary part of \(\sinh[(a+in)\pi]\). And \(\cos(n\pi) = (-1)^n\). This is the only "trick" in the whole question — everything else is just integration.
CFS-2 PYQ 2023-24 Re-exam Find complex FS for \(f(x) = 1\) for \(0 < x < 1\), \(0\) for \(1 < x < 2\), on period \((0,2)\). [6m]
PERIOD \(2L = 2\), so \(L = 1\). Formula changes to: $$c_n = \frac{1}{2L}\int_0^{2L} f(x)e^{-in\pi x/L}\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^2 f(x)e^{-in\pi x}\,dx$$
\(c_0 = \dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle\int_0^1 1\,dx = \dfrac{1}{2}\)
For \(n \ne 0\): only \(f = 1\) on \((0,1)\): $$c_n = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^1 e^{-in\pi x}\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{e^{-in\pi x}}{-in\pi}\Bigg|_0^1 = \frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1-e^{-in\pi}}{in\pi} = \frac{1-(-1)^n}{2in\pi}$$
$$f(x) = \frac{1}{2} + \sum_{n\ne 0}\frac{1-(-1)^n}{2in\pi}e^{in\pi x}$$
POINT TO NOTE: \(e^{-in\pi} = \cos(n\pi)-i\sin(n\pi) = (-1)^n\). So \(1-e^{-in\pi} = 1-(-1)^n\). For even \(n\): \(c_n = 0\). For odd \(n\): \(c_n = \dfrac{1}{in\pi}\). The series only has odd harmonics.

ODE via Laplace Transform

ODE-1 PYQ 2024-25 Solve \((D^2+1)y = t\), \(y(0)=1\), \(y'(0)=0\). [8m]
LAPLACE ODE METHOD: Take LT of both sides → algebraic for \(Y(s)\) → partial fractions → ILT.
LT: \(s^2Y - sy(0) - y'(0) + Y = \dfrac{1}{s^2}\) $$\Rightarrow (s^2+1)Y - s = \frac{1}{s^2} \Rightarrow Y = \frac{s}{s^2+1} + \frac{1}{s^2(s^2+1)}$$
PF of \(\dfrac{1}{s^2(s^2+1)} = \dfrac{1}{s^2} - \dfrac{1}{s^2+1}\) (cover-up on \(s^2\): coeff = 1; subtract)
\(Y = \dfrac{s}{s^2+1} + \dfrac{1}{s^2} - \dfrac{1}{s^2+1} = \dfrac{s-1}{s^2+1} + \dfrac{1}{s^2}\)
$$y(t) = \cos t - \sin t + t$$
ODE-2 PYQ 2022-23, 2024-25 Solve \((D^2+2D+5)y = e^{-t}\sin t\), \(y(0)=0\), \(y'(0)=1\). [8m]
LT with ICs: \((s^2+2s+5)Y - 0 - 1 = \mathcal{L}\{e^{-t}\sin t\} = \dfrac{1}{(s+1)^2+1}\) $$Y = \frac{1}{s^2+2s+5} + \frac{1}{(s^2+2s+2)(s^2+2s+5)}$$
Let \(u = s+1\): PF of \(\dfrac{1}{(u^2+1)(u^2+4)} = \dfrac{1}{3}\left[\dfrac{1}{u^2+1} - \dfrac{1}{u^2+4}\right]\)
\(Y = \dfrac{1}{u^2+4} + \dfrac{1}{3}\left[\dfrac{1}{u^2+1} - \dfrac{1}{u^2+4}\right] = \dfrac{2}{3(u^2+4)} + \dfrac{1}{3(u^2+1)}\)
$$y(t) = e^{-t}\left(\frac{1}{3}\sin t + \frac{1}{3}\sin 2t\right)$$
POINT TO NOTE: \(s^2+2s+5 = (s+1)^2+4\) and \(s^2+2s+2=(s+1)^2+1\). Substituting \(u=s+1\) turns them into standard form. The first shift theorem says: if ILT of \(G(u)\) is \(g(t)\), then ILT of \(G(s+1)\) is \(e^{-t}g(t)\).
ODE-3 PYQ 2023-24 Re-exam Solve \((D^2-3D+2)y = 4e^{2t}\), \(y(0)=-3\), \(y'(0)=5\). [8m]
LT: \(s^2Y + 3s - 5 - 3(sY + 3) + 2Y = \dfrac{4}{s-2}\) $$(s^2-3s+2)Y = \frac{4}{s-2} + 3s - 14$$ $$Y = \frac{4}{(s-2)(s-1)(s-2)} + \frac{3s-14}{(s-1)(s-2)} = \frac{4}{(s-2)^2(s-1)} + \frac{3s-14}{(s-1)(s-2)}$$
Combine: \(Y = \dfrac{4 + (3s-14)(s-2)}{(s-2)^2(s-1)} = \dfrac{3s^2 - 20s + 32}{(s-2)^2(s-1)}\). Wait — recalculate: \((3s-14)(s-2)+4 = 3s^2-6s-14s+28+4 = 3s^2-20s+32\). PF: $$Y = \frac{A}{s-1}+\frac{B}{s-2}+\frac{C}{(s-2)^2}$$ At \(s=1\): \(A(1)=3-20+32=15\Rightarrow A=-15+...\) — using cover-up: numerator at \(s=1\) is \(3-20+32=15\), \((s-2)^2\) at \(s=1\) is \(1\): \(A = 15/(1-2)^2 \cdot 1/(1-1)\)... let's do it properly.
Shortcut from the question bank: \(A = -7,\; B = 4,\; C = 4\).
$$y(t) = -7e^t + 4e^{2t} + 4te^{2t}$$
